## Preamble

This Policy Manual ("**Manual**") consolidates the human resources policies of Typhoon Electronics Solutions ("**the Company**", "**Typhoon Electronics**", "**Employer**") and shall apply to all Employees with effect from the Effective Date stated above. The Manual is issued under the authority of the Chief Executive Officer and supersedes all prior policies, circulars, oral understandings, or established practices on the matters covered herein.

This Manual is intended to comply with the provisions of the Tamil Nadu Shops & Establishments Act, 1947, the Maternity Benefit Act, 1961 (as amended), the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 ("**RPwD Act**"), and all other Central and State labour enactments as may apply to the Company from time to time.

The Company reserves the right to amend, modify, or rescind this Manual, or any part thereof, at any time, with or without prior notice. Any such modification shall be communicated through an official circular issued by the Human Resources function or the Chief Executive Officer.

## Scope and Applicability

This Manual shall apply to:

1. All persons employed by Typhoon Electronics on a full-time basis, whether on probation or confirmed;
2. Trainees and apprentices, save to the extent that any specific provision is rendered inapplicable by statute or by the express terms of their engagement;
3. Where expressly stated, contractors, consultants, interns, and visitors, in relation to those provisions that govern their conduct on Company premises or in connection with Company business.

Each individual to whom this Manual applies is hereinafter referred to as an "**Employee**", unless the context requires otherwise.

## Definitions

For the purposes of this Manual, the following terms shall bear the meanings ascribed to them below:

- **"Company"** means Typhoon Electronics Solutions, including its successors and assigns.
- **"Effective Date"** means 1 June 2026.
- **"Employee"** has the meaning given in the *Scope and Applicability* section above.
- **"HR"** or **"Human Resources"** means the function or person designated by the Company to administer this Manual.
- **"Management"** means the Chief Executive Officer and such other persons as the CEO may designate.
- **"Reporting Manager"** means the immediate supervisor of an Employee as recorded in the Company's records.
- **"Working Day"** means Monday through Friday between 9:30 AM and 6:30 PM, and Saturday between 9:30 AM and 1:00 PM, excluding declared public holidays.
- **"Calendar Year"** means the period commencing 1 January and ending 31 December.

# POLICY 1 — ATTENDANCE & LEAVE POLICY

## 1.1 Purpose

This Policy sets out the framework governing working hours, attendance, and the various categories of leave to which Employees are entitled, and the procedures for availing such leave.

## 1.2 Scope

This Policy applies to all Employees as defined in the *Scope and Applicability* section of this Manual.

## 1.3 Working Hours and Work Week

1.3.1 The Company observes a five-and-a-half (5.5) day work week.

1.3.2 Standard working hours shall be:

| Day | Working Hours |
|---|---|
| Monday – Friday | 9:30 AM to 6:30 PM (inclusive of a one-hour meal break) |
| Saturday | 9:30 AM to 1:00 PM (half day) |
| Sunday | Weekly Off |

1.3.3 Any change to the working hours or weekly off shall be at the discretion of Management and shall be notified in writing.

## 1.4 Attendance Recording

1.4.1 Attendance shall be recorded through the Company's designated attendance management system, presently **ClockBays**, or such other system as the Company may adopt from time to time.

1.4.2 Every Employee shall mark attendance accurately upon arrival and departure on each Working Day. Falsification of attendance records, including marking attendance on behalf of another Employee, shall constitute misconduct under the Code of Conduct.

1.4.3 A grace period of fifteen (15) minutes shall be permitted for late arrival. Late arrival beyond the grace period shall be recorded as "Late Mark". Three (3) Late Marks in any calendar month shall be treated as one (1) day of Casual Leave or, where leave balance is exhausted, as Loss of Pay.

## 1.5 Leave Categories and Annual Entitlements

1.5.1 Subject to the terms of this Policy, Employees shall be entitled to the following leave in each Calendar Year:

| Leave Category | Annual Entitlement | Accrual |
|---|---|---|
| Casual Leave (CL) | 24 days | 2 days per completed calendar month |
| Sick Leave (SL) | 10 days | Credited at the start of the Calendar Year (pro-rated for new joiners) |
| Maternity Leave | 26 weeks | Statutory, as per Maternity Benefit Act, 1961 (as amended) |
| Paternity Leave | 5 days | On the occasion of the birth of an Employee's child |
| Adoption Leave | As per Section 5(4) of the Maternity Benefit Act, 1961 | For Employees legally adopting a child below three months of age |
| Public Holidays | 15 days (per Annexure A) | Declared annually |

1.5.2 The Company does not offer Earned Leave or Privilege Leave. The entitlements at clause 1.5.1 represent the complete leave provision under this Policy.

## 1.6 Casual Leave — Carry-Forward and Lapse

1.6.1 Unused Casual Leave shall be carried forward to subsequent months within the same Calendar Year.

1.6.2 Any unused Casual Leave balance as at 31 March of each Calendar Year shall lapse and shall not be carried forward into the following Calendar Year.

1.6.3 No encashment shall be allowed in respect of Casual Leave or Sick Leave, whether during service or upon separation.

## 1.7 Sick Leave

1.7.1 Sick Leave shall be availed only in case of genuine illness or injury preventing the Employee from attending work.

1.7.2 For any Sick Leave exceeding two (2) consecutive Working Days, the Employee shall submit a medical certificate issued by a registered medical practitioner, failing which the absence shall be treated as Loss of Pay.

1.7.3 Sick Leave shall not be carried forward to the next Calendar Year and shall lapse if unused.

## 1.8 Maternity, Paternity, and Adoption Leave

1.8.1 **Maternity Leave** shall be granted in accordance with the Maternity Benefit Act, 1961 (as amended), which currently provides for twenty-six (26) weeks of paid leave for a woman Employee in respect of her first two children, and twelve (12) weeks in respect of subsequent children.

1.8.2 **Paternity Leave** of five (5) Working Days shall be granted to a male Employee on the occasion of the birth of his child. Such leave must be availed within thirty (30) days of the date of birth.

1.8.3 **Adoption Leave** shall be granted in accordance with the Maternity Benefit Act, 1961, namely up to twelve (12) weeks for an Employee who legally adopts a child below the age of three months, commencing from the date of handover of the child.

1.8.4 Application for Maternity, Paternity, or Adoption Leave shall be made in writing to HR, supported by such documentary evidence as may be reasonably required (birth certificate, adoption order, medical certificate, etc.).

## 1.9 Probation and Leave

1.9.1 The standard probation period for new Employees shall be three (3) months from the date of joining, save where otherwise specified in the Employee's appointment letter.

1.9.2 During the probation period, an Employee shall be entitled only to Sick Leave. Casual Leave shall not accrue or be available during probation.

1.9.3 Maternity Leave, Paternity Leave, and Adoption Leave shall be available during probation subject to the statutory eligibility conditions.

1.9.4 Casual Leave shall commence accruing prospectively from the date of confirmation, in accordance with clause 1.5.1.

## 1.10 Application and Approval of Leave

1.10.1 **Planned Leave.** Applications for Casual Leave shall be submitted in writing (including by email or through the HR system) to the Employee's Reporting Manager, with HR in copy, at least seven (7) calendar days in advance of the proposed date of absence.

1.10.2 **Sick Leave.** An Employee unable to attend work due to illness shall notify the Reporting Manager and HR at the earliest opportunity on the day of absence, and shall submit a formal leave application immediately upon return to work.

1.10.3 **Approval Authority.** All leave is subject to the prior approval of the Reporting Manager. HR shall maintain the official leave record. The Reporting Manager may, in the interest of business continuity, decline or defer a leave request, communicating the reasons in writing.

1.10.4 **Emergency Leave.** Where an Employee is unable to provide advance notice due to genuine emergency, the Employee shall communicate with the Reporting Manager at the earliest possible time, and shall regularise the absence on return.

## 1.11 Half-Days, Loss of Pay, and Saturday Treatment

1.11.1 A "half-day" shall mean an absence or attendance of four (4) hours, calculated against the standard daily working hours.

1.11.2 **Loss of Pay (LOP).** Where an Employee has exhausted the applicable leave balance, any further absence shall be treated as Loss of Pay. LOP shall be calculated on a per-day basis at the rate of (Monthly Gross Salary ÷ Total Days in the Month).

1.11.3 **Saturday Absence.** As Saturday is a half-day, an absence on Saturday shall be debited as one-half (0.5) day of leave, or one-half (0.5) day of Loss of Pay, as the case may be.

## 1.12 Public Holidays

The list of declared public holidays for the relevant Calendar Year shall be published by HR. The list for Calendar Year 2026 is set out at **Annexure A**.

# POLICY 2 — TRAVEL POLICY

## 2.1 Purpose

This Policy governs business travel undertaken by Employees on behalf of the Company, including pre-approval, entitlements, and the reimbursement of related expenses.

## 2.2 Scope

This Policy applies to all Employees authorised to undertake business travel on behalf of the Company, whether domestic or international.

## 2.3 Pre-Approval

2.3.1 No Employee shall undertake business travel without prior written approval from the Reporting Manager and the Chief Executive Officer (or such authority as the CEO may designate).

2.3.2 The request for approval shall be submitted by email and shall include:

(a) Purpose and business justification of the travel;
(b) Destination, dates of departure and return;
(c) Mode of travel (flight, train, road) and class proposed;
(d) Accommodation proposed;
(e) An estimated budget covering travel, stay, food, and incidentals.

2.3.3 Travel shall be undertaken strictly in accordance with the approved estimate. Any material deviation from the approved estimate shall require fresh approval before incurring the additional expense.

## 2.4 Entitlements

2.4.1 Class of travel, accommodation category, and other entitlements shall be as per the **approved travel estimate** for each trip, having regard to the nature and purpose of the travel, the destination, and the Employee's role.

2.4.2 The Company shall publish a detailed grade-wise entitlement matrix at a later stage. Until such matrix is published, entitlements shall be determined on a per-trip basis through the pre-approval process at clause 2.3.

## 2.5 Reimbursement Model

2.5.1 The Company shall reimburse business travel expenses on the following basis:

| Head of Expense | Basis of Reimbursement |
|---|---|
| Travel (flight, train, road) | Actuals, against original tickets/bills |
| Accommodation (Stay) | Actuals, against original hotel invoice |
| Local Transport (cabs, autos) | Actuals, against receipts or trip records |
| Food / Meals | Fixed daily allowance of ₹ 450 per person per day, in lieu of bills |
| Other approved expenses | Actuals, against original bills |

2.5.2 The food allowance at ₹ 450 per person per day shall apply uniformly and shall not require submission of bills. Any expenditure on food in excess of this allowance shall be borne by the Employee.

2.5.3 The Company shall reimburse expenses on Stay, Food, and Transport. Heads not expressly listed (such as personal entertainment, alcoholic beverages, mini-bar charges, laundry for short trips, and similar items) shall not be reimbursable.

## 2.6 Submission of Documents

2.6.1 All claims for reimbursement shall be supported by original receipts, invoices, or tickets, except where the head of expense is reimbursed on a per-diem basis under clause 2.5.

2.6.2 Submission of any forged, altered, or duplicated bill shall be treated as serious misconduct and shall attract action under the Code of Conduct.

## 2.7 Claim Submission Timeline

2.7.1 An Employee shall submit the travel claim, together with all supporting documents, within ten (10) calendar days of the date of return from travel.

2.7.2 Claims received after the above timeline may be rejected at the discretion of HR or Management, save where the delay is justified.

## 2.8 Reimbursement Payment

2.8.1 Approved travel reimbursements shall be credited to the Employee along with the subsequent salary cycle following approval of the claim.

2.8.2 In cases of genuine emergency or financial hardship, an Employee may make a written request for expedited processing, which shall be considered by HR and Management on a case-by-case basis.

## 2.9 Travel Advances

2.9.1 Travel advances shall not ordinarily be issued.

2.9.2 In cases of emergency or where the nature of travel reasonably warrants an advance (e.g., international travel, extended duration travel), an Employee may, on written request supported by the approved estimate, be granted an advance at the discretion of Management.

2.9.3 The advance shall be reconciled against actual expenses upon the Employee's return, and any unspent balance shall be remitted to the Company without delay.

# POLICY 3 — DRESS CODE POLICY

## 3.1 Purpose

This Policy prescribes the standards of attire to be observed by Employees, to maintain a professional appearance consistent with the Company's image and the expectations of clients and visitors.

## 3.2 Standard Dress Code

3.2.1 The dress code applicable on Working Days shall be as follows:

| Day | Dress Code |
|---|---|
| Monday – Friday | **Formal attire**, with closed-toe formal shoes mandatory |
| Saturday | **Business Casual**, including jeans and polo t-shirts |

3.2.2 **Formal Attire** means, without limitation:

(a) For men: full-sleeve formal shirt, formal trousers, and formal closed-toe shoes; tie and blazer optional but encouraged for senior or client-facing roles.
(b) For women: formal trousers/skirt with formal shirt or blouse, formal Indian wear (saree, salwar-kameez, or equivalent), and formal footwear.

3.2.3 **Business Casual** means, without limitation, clean and presentable clothing including collared shirts, polo t-shirts, jeans (in good condition, without rips or tears), and casual footwear. Sportswear, sleeveless tops, shorts, slippers, and flip-flops are not permitted.

## 3.3 Client Meetings and Visitor Days

3.3.1 Formal attire as defined at clause 3.2.2 shall be mandatory for all client meetings, regardless of the day of the week, unless the client's own dress code or the nature of the meeting indicates otherwise.

3.3.2 Where a client visit or external visitor is scheduled on a Saturday, Employees who are likely to interact with the client or visitor shall observe formal attire in place of the Saturday business casual standard.

## 3.4 Grooming and Hygiene

Employees shall maintain a clean and well-groomed appearance at all times. Personal hygiene, neatness of clothing, and general presentability shall be considered part of the dress code under this Policy.

## 3.5 Non-Compliance

3.5.1 An Employee found in non-compliance with this Policy may be required to return home and change, with the absence treated as half-day Casual Leave or Loss of Pay, as applicable.

3.5.2 Repeated non-compliance shall be treated as misconduct and shall attract action under the Code of Conduct.

# POLICY 4 — CODE OF CONDUCT

## 4.1 Purpose

This Code of Conduct ("**Code**") sets forth the standards of professional and ethical behaviour expected of every Employee of Typhoon Electronics. Adherence to the Code is a condition of employment.

## 4.2 Core Values

The Company is built on, and committed to upholding, the following core values, which every Employee is expected to embody:

1. **Integrity** — honesty, transparency, and consistency between word and deed in all dealings.
2. **Respect** — treating colleagues, clients, vendors, and visitors with dignity and courtesy.
3. **Accountability** — owning one's responsibilities, decisions, and their consequences.
4. **Customer Focus** — placing the needs and trust of the customer at the centre of every action.
5. **Innovation** — seeking better ways to do things, and welcoming considered change.
6. **Teamwork** — collaborating constructively, sharing knowledge, and supporting one another.
7. **Safety** — protecting oneself, colleagues, and Company property from harm.
8. **Quality** — delivering work that meets professional standards and stands the test of scrutiny.

## 4.3 Conflict of Interest

4.3.1 Every Employee shall act in the best interests of the Company and shall avoid any situation in which personal interests, whether financial or otherwise, conflict, or may reasonably be perceived to conflict, with the interests of the Company.

4.3.2 Without limiting the generality of the foregoing, Employees shall disclose to HR, in writing and at the earliest opportunity, any of the following:

(a) Direct or indirect financial interest in any vendor, customer, competitor, or business partner of the Company;
(b) Engagement of a relative as a vendor, contractor, or consultant of the Company;
(c) Personal relationships (familial or romantic) with a Reporting Manager or a direct report;
(d) Any outside engagement that may give rise to a conflict (see clause 4.9 on Moonlighting).

4.3.3 The Company shall determine, in consultation with the Employee, appropriate steps to manage any disclosed conflict.

## 4.4 Gifts and Anti-Bribery

4.4.1 No Employee shall offer, give, solicit, or accept, directly or indirectly, any bribe, kickback, illegal gratification, or other improper inducement, whether in cash or in kind, in connection with the business of the Company.

4.4.2 Modest gifts of nominal value (such as customary festival sweets, branded merchandise, or stationery) given or received in the ordinary course of business may be acceptable, provided they:

(a) Do not exceed a value of ₹ 2,500 per occasion;
(b) Are not in the form of cash or cash equivalents;
(c) Are not solicited;
(d) Could not reasonably be perceived to influence business decisions.

4.4.3 Gifts exceeding the threshold at clause 4.4.2 shall be declared to HR and shall not be retained without written approval.

4.4.4 This clause shall be read in conjunction with the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988, and all other applicable anti-corruption legislation.

## 4.5 Confidentiality and Intellectual Property

4.5.1 Every Employee shall maintain in strict confidence all Confidential Information of the Company, of its clients, and of its business partners, both during and after the term of employment.

4.5.2 "**Confidential Information**" includes, without limitation: technical designs and specifications; software code; client lists, pricing, and contracts; financial information; business plans and strategies; trade secrets; vendor information; personnel information; and any information marked as confidential or which a reasonable person would understand to be confidential.

4.5.3 All intellectual property created by an Employee in the course of, or in connection with, employment with the Company — including inventions, designs, software, written materials, processes, and improvements — shall be the sole and exclusive property of the Company. The Employee shall execute such assignments, declarations, and other documents as the Company may reasonably require to vest such intellectual property in the Company.

4.5.4 Upon separation from the Company, the Employee shall return all Confidential Information and intellectual property in any form (physical, digital, or otherwise) without retaining any copies.

## 4.6 IT and Acceptable Use

4.6.1 Company-issued computers, network access, email accounts, software licences, and other information technology assets ("**IT Assets**") shall be used primarily for legitimate business purposes. Incidental personal use is permitted provided it does not interfere with work or violate this Policy.

4.6.2 Employees shall not:

(a) Install unauthorised software on Company IT Assets;
(b) Access, download, distribute, or store unlawful, obscene, defamatory, or infringing material;
(c) Attempt to bypass security controls or access systems or data without authorisation;
(d) Share login credentials with any other person;
(e) Use Company IT Assets to conduct any business unrelated to the Company;
(f) Disable or tamper with security software, monitoring tools, or audit logs.

4.6.3 The Company reserves the right to monitor, inspect, and audit the use of Company IT Assets, including email and internet usage, in accordance with applicable law. Employees shall have no expectation of privacy in respect of communications or data on Company IT Assets.

## 4.7 Social Media

4.7.1 Employees shall exercise discretion and good judgement in their use of social media, whether personal or professional.

4.7.2 An Employee shall not, on any social media platform:

(a) Disclose Confidential Information of the Company or its clients;
(b) Make statements that bring, or are likely to bring, the Company, its Employees, clients, or business partners into disrepute;
(c) Impersonate the Company or speak on its behalf without authorisation;
(d) Post discriminatory, harassing, defamatory, obscene, or threatening content directed at any person, including colleagues.

4.7.3 Where an Employee identifies their association with the Company on a personal social media profile, the Employee shall make it clear that views expressed are personal and do not represent the views of the Company.

## 4.8 Substance / Alcohol at the Workplace

4.8.1 The consumption of alcoholic beverages, narcotic drugs, or psychotropic substances on Company premises or during working hours is strictly prohibited.

4.8.2 No Employee shall report to work, or remain at work, in a state of intoxication or under the influence of any substance that impairs the Employee's ability to perform duties safely and competently.

4.8.3 Consumption of alcohol at Company-authorised events (such as celebrations, off-sites, or client functions) is permitted in moderation, and Employees shall continue to be bound by the standards of conduct in this Code at such events.

## 4.9 Moonlighting / Outside Employment

4.9.1 An Employee shall not, during the term of employment with the Company, engage in any other employment, business, profession, consultancy, or paid engagement, whether full-time or part-time, without the prior written approval of the Company.

4.9.2 An application for such approval shall disclose the nature, duration, and remuneration of the proposed outside engagement, and shall confirm that it does not, and will not, conflict with the Employee's duties to the Company, compete with the Company, or involve the use of Company resources or Confidential Information.

4.9.3 Pro bono, academic, or charitable activities that do not impair the Employee's performance and do not give rise to a conflict shall not require approval but should be disclosed for transparency.

## 4.10 Workplace Behaviour and Harassment

4.10.1 Every Employee shall conduct themselves with courtesy, dignity, and professionalism in all interactions with colleagues, clients, vendors, and visitors.

4.10.2 The following conduct is strictly prohibited:

(a) Bullying, intimidation, or coercion of any colleague;
(b) Verbal abuse, threats, or use of offensive language;
(c) Physical violence or any threat thereof;
(d) Sexual harassment;
(e) Discrimination on any protected ground (see Policy 5);
(f) Retaliation against any person who has raised a complaint or supported an investigation.

4.10.3 Any Employee who believes they have witnessed or experienced prohibited conduct under this clause is encouraged to report it to HR.

## 4.11 Punctuality and Attendance Conduct

4.11.1 Every Employee shall observe the working hours stipulated under Policy 1 and shall be punctual in commencing work, attending meetings, and meeting deadlines.

4.11.2 Habitual absenteeism, unauthorised absence, or repeated late arrival shall constitute misconduct and shall be addressed under the disciplinary procedure at clause 4.13.

## 4.12 Use of Company Property

4.12.1 Company property, including IT Assets, office equipment, vehicles, premises, and supplies, shall be used solely for legitimate business purposes and shall be maintained with reasonable care.

4.12.2 No Employee shall remove Company property from the premises without authorisation, nor use Company property for personal gain or for the benefit of any third party.

4.12.3 Upon separation, the Employee shall return all Company property in good working condition, subject to normal wear and tear.

## 4.13 Disciplinary Procedure

4.13.1 Where an Employee is alleged to have committed misconduct or breached this Code or any other policy of the Company, the following procedure shall be observed.

4.13.2 **Stages of Disciplinary Action.** The Company shall, depending on the gravity and the recurrence of the conduct, take action through one or more of the following stages:

| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| Stage 1 | **Verbal Warning** — issued by the Reporting Manager and recorded in the Employee's file by HR |
| Stage 2 | **Written Warning** — issued by HR or Management, requiring acknowledgement by the Employee |
| Stage 3 | **Final Written Warning** — issued by Management, with explicit notice that further violation may lead to termination |
| Stage 4 | **Suspension or Termination** — imposed by Management following due inquiry |

4.13.3 **Right to Be Heard.** Before any action at Stage 2 or higher is taken, the Employee shall:

(a) Be issued a show-cause notice in writing setting out the alleged misconduct;
(b) Be given a reasonable opportunity, of not less than three (3) Working Days, to submit a written response;
(c) Where the matter is contested or its facts are not clear, be given an opportunity to be heard in person.

4.13.4 **Gross Misconduct.** Notwithstanding the staged approach at clause 4.13.2, conduct constituting gross misconduct (including but not limited to theft, fraud, falsification of records, physical violence, sexual harassment, serious breach of confidentiality, or insubordination) may attract direct action at Stage 4, subject to the right to be heard under clause 4.13.3.

4.13.5 **Suspension Pending Inquiry.** Where the Company is of the view that the continued presence of the Employee may prejudice an investigation or pose a risk, the Employee may be placed on suspension pending inquiry. Suspension shall not by itself be regarded as a finding of guilt.

4.13.6 **Appeal.** An Employee aggrieved by any disciplinary action at Stage 2 or higher may, within seven (7) Working Days of receiving notice of the action, prefer a written appeal to the Chief Executive Officer (or, where the original action was taken by the CEO, to a senior person nominated by the CEO). The decision on appeal shall be final and binding.

# POLICY 5 — EQUAL OPPORTUNITY & ANTI-DISCRIMINATION POLICY

## 5.1 Purpose

Typhoon Electronics is committed to providing a workplace free from discrimination and harassment, and to ensuring equality of opportunity in all aspects of employment. This Policy sets out the Company's commitment, the prohibited conduct, and the mechanisms for redress in respect of discrimination and all kinds of harassment.

## 5.2 Scope

This Policy applies to all Employees as defined in the *Scope and Applicability* section of this Manual, and, where the context requires, to applicants for employment and to contractors, vendors' personnel, interns, and visitors in their dealings with the Company.

## 5.3 Equal Opportunity Commitment

5.3.1 The Company shall provide equal opportunity to all Employees and applicants in all matters relating to employment, including (but not limited to) recruitment, terms of employment, remuneration, training, promotion, transfer, discipline, and separation.

5.3.2 No decision affecting employment shall be made on the basis of any of the following **protected characteristics**:

(a) Gender;
(b) Religion;
(c) Caste;
(d) Disability;
(e) Age;
(f) Marital status;
(g) Sexual orientation;
(h) Place of origin;
(i) Pregnancy or maternity status;
(j) Language.

5.3.3 The Company's commitment to equal opportunity is informed by the constitutional principles of equality, the RPwD Act, and the spirit of Indian labour legislation.

## 5.4 Prohibited Conduct

5.4.1 The following are strictly prohibited:

(a) **Discrimination** — any less favourable treatment of a person on any protected ground listed at clause 5.3.2;
(b) **Harassment** — any unwelcome conduct that has the purpose or effect of creating an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating, or offensive environment, save where such conduct constitutes sexual harassment;
(c) **Victimisation or retaliation** — any adverse action against a person for raising a complaint, supporting another's complaint, or participating in an inquiry.

## 5.5 Reasonable Accommodation

5.5.1 The Company shall, to the extent reasonable and practicable, provide accommodation to Employees and applicants with disabilities, having regard to the nature of the role, the workplace environment, and the resources of the Company.

5.5.2 Reasonable accommodation may include, where appropriate:

(a) Modifications to the physical workspace to ensure accessibility;
(b) Provision of assistive technology;
(c) Adjustments to working hours or schedules;
(d) Modification of duties, where consistent with the requirements of the role;
(e) Such other adjustments as may reasonably be required.

5.5.3 An Employee seeking accommodation under this clause may make a written request to HR, which shall be considered in good faith and in consultation with the Employee.

## 5.6 Complaint Procedure

5.6.1 Any Employee who believes they have experienced or witnessed discrimination, non-sexual harassment, or retaliation under this Policy may make a complaint in writing to HR or to the Chief Executive Officer. 

5.6.2 The Company shall:

(a) Acknowledge receipt of the complaint promptly;
(b) Investigate the complaint impartially and confidentially;
(c) Provide both the complainant and the respondent a fair opportunity to be heard;
(d) Take appropriate action, including action under the Code of Conduct, where the complaint is substantiated;
(e) Communicate the outcome to the parties.

5.6.3 Complaints shall be treated as confidential to the maximum extent possible, save as required for the proper conduct of the inquiry or by law.
